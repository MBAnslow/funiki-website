We will dive into feedback from participants across two sessions to provide a broad qualitative analysis. Questions were posed to the group as a whole with plenty of space for open-ended responses. We recorded the audio on a mobile phone and used Vibe to create transcriptions which we then verified by hand.

It's worth broadly characterising our participants first. Most participants hadn't tried table-top RPG sessions before and came from a technical background. It's hard to disentangle the Funiki-specific enjoyment from the general table-top RPG experience. Furthermore, participants were probably more likely to be interested in technology and forgiving to operating at the bleeding edge of technology.

## Related to Sound

In general they wanted more sound effects and, in particular, surround sound.

> _The sound was not, uh, loud enough, you know._
>
> _Suffices to put you in the experience_
>
> _It's, it could be like crazy._
>
> _But with the surround sound._

Though we thought that sounds were a more literal representation there was still plenty of space for imagination. In the scene outside the lighthouse we were only playing the sound of waves. However, in the discussion participants had heard or remembered sounds that weren't there.

> Was there like rain? Was it raining?

> Maybe in terms of sound, we could have had more. I don't remember if there was wind or not. Maybe we could have had some wind.

This could also be because the general mono sound of waves is somehow in the general semantic field of 'water' and the speaker was placed between participants rather than at the location one would expect waves to be (somewhere at the periphery). Combined with other light and narrative cues it just becomes another hint at the composition of the scene.

## Physicality

### Making sense of the lights

In the discussion it was clear that participants were trying to piece together the physicality of the scene. In particular, within the lighthouse, the participants could still see the lighthouse light spinning around but then they asked themselves, why are they seeing this from inside the lighthouse similar to the outside?

> Especially I love the lighthouse. Yeah, it's nice. The only thing with that is if we are inside the lighthouse, then why do we see it? Maybe because we only have one window and so we see the light when it comes on the side of the window. That's what I tried to do. I could have a little light of everything.

### Placement on a map

This topic also came up within the lighthouse where they questioned which floor they were on. 

> I wasn't really well able to know at which floor we were. That was one thing. Apart from that, uh, like when we knew, I could see the room and everything, but, uh, I wasn't always aware, uh, at which point of the lighthouse we were.

Which is interesting. 

Exposed to a generic location like a lighthouse, it's easy to imagine yourself at that location. There is also probably only one door one would imagine (the front door) which anchors the imagination of participants. But when it comes to building a finer-grained map, more precision is required.

### The Size of Spaces

Participants described their imagined 'size' of the first room in the lighthouse.

> I feel like the living room is pretty large. It's like a castle. Now when I'm thinking, it's actually just like a lighthouse. To me, I really imagined that you had the lighthouse and you had like a very little house attached to the lighthouse and the living room was in this little house.

> It's true that in my head the living room was very big. I don't know why I had that impression. To me too then. That's why I didn't think we were right inside the lighthouse but like next week. And then to go see the, we entered the stairs of the lighthouse. And then when the light goes out. The light goes out and then there was like the, the commences. Yeah, it was great. That was cool. That was scary actually.

In our physical space were were in a fixed size room and our lights can't change that too much. That is particularly true when the room is fully illuminated. With the candle light effect the borders of the room become dark which makes it easier to imagine that the room is larger, but ultimately individuals perceived very different sized rooms when the room was entirely lit.
